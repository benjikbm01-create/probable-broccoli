# Technical report — what was built and what was actually tested

## Architecture (short)
- **A world save + one Fabric mod** (plus Fabric API). A mod was necessary because Minecraft 1.21.1 has no way to give individual humanoid characters their own skins without client code. Once the mod existed, the story systems went in it too, as a small data-driven engine:
  - **Content is data:** dialogue, quests, hints, schedules, books and journal entries are JSON under `data/forgotten_island/fi/`, loaded with the normal datapack reload.
  - **A tiny condition/effect language** drives progression. Everything is server-authoritative. The client only displays things and reports choices.
- **Residents** are a custom `PathAwareEntity` drawn with the full player model (both skin layers, slim or wide arms, per-character height). They have head tracking, walking, sitting, sleeping in beds, a working animation and overhead conversation lines.
  - A *director* keeps one live entity per character near the player, at the place their daily schedule says. Entities aren't saved with chunks, so they can never duplicate or get lost.
- **The island** is generated by code (`build/`): a pure-Java terrain model with handcrafted pads, a stream and waterfall, cliff roughening, about 14 village structures with interiors, the forest sites, the Keep and the Undercroft. Vanilla tree features are used for 1,137 trees. Building takes about 20 seconds on a dedicated server; the result is shipped as a normal world save.
- **Protection:** Adventure mode, block-break/interaction guards, mob griefing and fire spread off, keep-inventory on, checkpoints, quest-item recovery, and encounter/boss resets on death.

### What the unit tests cover
- Condition language parsing and evaluation; computed variables (`dating`, `secrets`); relationship clamping; reset keeping dev mode.
- **Save/load:** NBT round trip of the full story state, including relationships.
- **Puzzles:** the Singing Stones order logic; the mirror puzzle has exactly one solution and starts unsolved; the gliding-stone puzzle is solvable in exactly 11 pushes and starts unsolved.
- **Content validation:**
  - every dialogue/quest/schedule reference resolves (places, items, events);
  - every dialogue node is reachable;
  - every flag a condition tests can actually be set somewhere (typo detector);
  - main objectives chain one into the next, with three side quests and three hint tiers each;
  - at least 10 secrets, each with a world zone;
  - schedules cover all 24 hours;
  - every resident has dialogue, a schedule and a 64×64 skin that uses the overlay layer.
- **Story routes, played through the real dialogue files:**
  - main story with no romance (with both endings of the final conversation);
  - dating one character;
  - dating two (including the agreement scene with the first partner);
  - **dating all three at once**, with the group epilogue;
  - friend-only paths (romance options don't appear without a romantic choice);
  - all three side quests.

### What the in-world self-test covers
These run on the real built island through the game's own code:
- **Content and rules:** live content validity; key game rules.
- **Places and gates:** every NPC schedule spot is physically usable (floor, headroom, bed or seat); every gate's blocks match its saved state.
- **Puzzles, on the actual blocks:**
  - Singing Stones: silent before the song, wrong order resets, correct order opens the thorn wall physically, reset re-closes it.
  - Sun Court: rotating the real mirror blocks solves it and opens the tower.
  - Gliding stones: pushing the real rune stones with a solver plan opens the vault; the in-room reset can't undo a solved puzzle; a full reset restores everything.
- **Secrets and items:** secret discovery and counting; quest items returned when lost; enchanted rewards.
- **People and dialogue:** live dialogue sessions for all seven residents; quest chain m1 → m7 → complete.
- **Encounters:** the guards fight spawns, completes when all guards are dead, and resets cleanly on death.
- **Boss:** spawns, the arena closes, abort/death reset reopens it, phase-two adds appear, victory is recorded, the arena reopens, the Ember is received, the shortcut opens, and the Ember relights the lantern (beacon).
- **Residents:** all are custom entities (no villagers or traders in the world); each visits at least four places a day; walking paths between nearby schedule spots, re-planned like the runtime does (24/29 walkable — the rest are reached by design as described in `KNOWN_ISSUES.md`).
- **Reset:** clears progress and closes all gates.

### What the client end-to-end run covers
A scripted player, using real client input (interaction packets, dialogue clicks, block use, chest looting, combat), played:
- **Act one:** the title/intro screen; the arrival; conversations with four residents through the dialogue UI; the journal and hints.
- **The forest:** learning the song; the Singing Stones via their buttons; a full romance through the UI, ending in the night-time date and becoming a couple (verified server-side as `dating=1`).
- **The Keep:** the Sun Court mirrors; climbing to the lantern; looting the key; unlocking the cistern.
- **The Undercroft:** three memory scenes; the gliding stones pushed through the block interaction.
- **The finale:** the boss fight; the Warden's last words; relighting the lantern with the Ember; the festival; the final conversation; the credits screen; the post-game notice.

19 screenshots were captured and reviewed (in `mod/run-client/screenshots/`, not committed).

### Visual verification
Screenshots were taken and inspected throughout development (village, harbour, square, chapel, inn interior, forest, stones, Keep, courtyard, Undercroft, Wellspring, residents at their schedule spots, every UI screen). Problems found this way and fixed include:
- a cliff face that looked artificial;
- speckled soil;
- blurred journal pages;
- an overflowing dialogue menu;
- a clipped title;
- pop-up toasts at the climax.

## Not tested
Sound (no audio device), real GPUs, the official launcher, other mods, and human playtesting. See `KNOWN_ISSUES.md`.
